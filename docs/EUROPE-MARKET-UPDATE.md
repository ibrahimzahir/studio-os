## Independent supporting evidence

Eurostat's 2026 publication reports 2025 online-shopping participation among people aged 16–74 at roughly 95% in Ireland, 94% in the Netherlands and 91% in Denmark. These measure all online goods/services, not downloadable planners.

Its e-commerce analysis reports e-book/e-audio purchasing in the relevant recent-internet-user survey population: Ireland 25% and Denmark 23% in 2025. This is closer evidence of paying for digital content, but still does not establish demand for our specific PDF/toolkit. For broad online shopping among internet users, ages 25–34 and 35–44 show 90% and 87% participation respectively. These are not PDF-buyer age distributions.

## Revised test priorities — hypotheses, not rankings of proven profitability

| Market | Reason to test | Product preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Ireland | English-language entry; strong online/digital purchase participation | A4, euro, relevant terminology; locally appropriate units/content. |
| Netherlands | High online purchase participation; specifically proposed in video | Test English creative rather than assume acceptance; compare native-reviewed Dutch localization. |
| Sweden | Specifically proposed in video; separate Nordic market | Test English versus native-reviewed Swedish; A4, SEK, appropriate local examples. |
| Denmark | Strong digital-book participation in Eurostat | Consider a separate test after an offer works; native-reviewed Danish where needed. |
| UK | English-language European market; separate from EU | GBP, A4, UK terminology. Do not apply EU rules indiscriminately. |

Germany and France are further localization candidates, not automatically lower-competition markets. Market size, conversion, competition, payment methods and local trust matter. Lower English fluency or local relevance can outweigh cheap ads.

Start with one offer and one or two geographies, not all countries simultaneously. For a household/life-event product, test a defined life situation rather than treating every person aged 25–44 as the customer. For a business operations tool, occupation and workflow are more relevant than age alone.

## Economics and safeguards

Compare acquisition cost per paid order, contribution after fees/refunds/support, and conversion, not only CPM. Hypothetical example: a $0.30 click at 0.5% paid conversion costs $60 per order; a $0.80 click at 4% costs $20. Cheap reach need not be profitable reach.

No ad budget, platform connection, customer outreach or product release was authorized by this research. Prepare country-specific drafts and experiments; the owner approves spending. Verify applicable consumer, digital-content tax and privacy obligations before sales with the relevant official guidance; do not copy US legal/tax assumptions into EU products.

## Studio OS implications

Keep market country, audience, language, currency, units, paper size, acquisition channel, evidence source/date and measured results distinct. Rank opportunities by product × geography × channel, rather than issuing one worldwide niche score. Pinterest organic distribution and paid Meta acquisition are different experiments; the video's paid-ad argument is not a Pinterest country-performance measurement.
