# Buyer research: digital products in the AI era

Update: read EUROPE-MARKET-UPDATE.md alongside this screen. Direct video transcript review and Eurostat evidence support European test audiences; US marketplace size alone does not determine best acquisition economics.

Researched 24 November 2026. This replaces unsupported confidence in earlier niche scores. It is an initial public-source screen, not sales validation of our own products. No interviews, outreach, paid campaigns or purchases were conducted.

## Verdict

Generic AI-written PDFs have weak differentiation. There is evidence that people still buy specific downloadable organizers and editable templates, including recent item-level reviews. That does not establish our ability to acquire customers profitably. A useful product can be a PDF, spreadsheet or interactive tool; file format is not a selling proposition.

Earlier $500–$1,000 income targets and 75–90% repetitive-work automation estimates were scenarios, not measured forecasts. Neither the studio dashboard nor a daily content factory validates demand. Do not present a niche score as a probability of sales.

## Evidence of actual purchases and useful outcomes

These are public listing observations, not an audit of Etsy transactions. Item reviews are stronger purchase signals than listings alone. Review counts are cumulative and must not be converted into monthly sales/revenue. Seller location, URL locale and shop-wide sales do not establish buyer demographics.

1. **Vacation-rental welcome guides.** [SoraCollective's editable welcome-book listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1421073272/airbnb-welcome-book-template-airbnb?lang_mismatch=1) displayed 216 item reviews, including 2026 reviews about customization and practical hosting use. One reviewer described using it for a rental in Idaho. Another wanted a contact detail updated across pages automatically: useful product feedback, not a market-size estimate.
2. **Caregiver organization.** [CompetentCaregiver's binder](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1185690550/caregiver-binder-medical-home-organizer) displayed 109 item reviews, including 2026 comments about comprehensive organization and coordinating care. This supports investigating organizational value, not selling medical advice.
3. **Household planning.** [MyProductiveLifeCo's chore spreadsheet](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1408710975/cleaning-chore-list-spreadsheet-google) displayed 50 item reviews, with 2026 comments about usability, organization and reduced overwhelm. This is a household product, not evidence for a cleaning-business toolkit. It also illustrates low-price competition.
4. **Mobile detailing remains weakly evidenced in this sample.** [This $7.99 detailing workflow](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4556760752/mobile-auto-detailing-business-forms) explicitly had no item reviews. Its shop-wide sales are not sales of that listing. [Another detailing starter kit](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4578810324/mobile-detailing-business-starter-kit) also lacked reviews in the retrieved page. These observations prove competing supply, not strong demand or a failed category.

The sample was deliberately small and includes both positive and weak evidence. Established reviewed listings can introduce survivorship bias. Historical reviews do not reveal customer acquisition costs, refunds, current monthly sales or how many new entrants fail.

## Buyer hypotheses to test

**The ages below are proposed recruitment/targeting bands, not measured ages of digital-product buyers.** No reliable PDF-specific age distribution was found. Do not infer ages from reviewer names, photographs or AI adoption statistics.

| Buyer | Initial geography | Proposed age band | Product/outcome | Why buy instead of prompt? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent short-term-rental host | US first; UK/Canada/Australia exploratory | 30–60 | Editable guest guide + property operations checklist | Coherent design, ready structure, quick customization, fewer repeated guest questions. |
| Owner of a small cleaning/detailing service | US first | 25–55 | Tested job-costing/quote spreadsheet + operating forms | Usable formulas, realistic units, error handling, clear workflow and support. Demand for our exact offer remains unvalidated. |
| Adult coordinating family care | US first | 40–64 | Fillable handover/contact/appointment organizer | Coordination under time pressure; reusable, readable forms. Keep it organizational and do not give clinical advice. |

US priority is supported only by broad marketplace spending. UK, Canada and Australia are language/localization hypotheses, not countries proven to convert for our products. Localize terminology, paper sizes, currency and units before expansion. Do not ship US legal/tax templates into another jurisdiction unchanged.

Maldives can be an accessible pilot for guesthouse operations IF the owner can reach operators directly. That is a distribution hypothesis; it is not evidence of a large downloadable-template market. Firsthand access and paid feedback may be more valuable than anonymous international traffic.

## Why paying can still make sense

A buyer may know AI and still prefer buying because the cost of prompting, checking formulas, designing, editing and troubleshooting exceeds the price of a ready-to-use result. We must demonstrate that advantage with a live preview, example inputs/outputs, independent QA and relevant instructions. These are proposed value mechanisms supported directionally by buyer reviews; actual time savings require measurement.

Strong offers: tested calculators, editable operational systems, genuinely useful personalization, clear setup and support. Weak offers: generic ebooks, unverified checklists, repetitive planners, prompt collections and automatically generated bulk content with no unique use.

AI also helps competitors. Our use of AI is not a defensible advantage by itself. Distribution, domain understanding, reliability and customer feedback matter more than production volume.

## What would establish earning potential for us

1. Choose ONE buyer and workflow; start with a host operations product or a service-business costing tool according to reachable buyers.
2. Observe 8–10 users doing the task; ask about current methods and past purchases rather than hypothetical enthusiasm. Outreach needs owner authorization.
3. Demonstrate a small working sample and offer a paid pilot. Seek 3–5 independent paying customers before expanding the catalog. These are proposed experiment gates, not statistical proof.
4. Record qualified visits, paid conversion, support time, refunds, acquisition costs, fees and contribution margin. Interview failures and nonbuyers too.
5. Improve or stop based on results. Do not claim a fixed conversion threshold is universal.

Illustrative arithmetic only: $25 × 20 orders = $500 gross revenue; $25 × 40 = $1,000 gross. This is not profit or MRR. Fees, refunds, acquisition, hosting/API costs and support reduce it. No current evidence predicts we will obtain those orders.

Before selling, verify a lawful payout/storefront route from the Maldives. Etsy is research evidence here, not a confirmed sales channel. [Official supported-country list](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015710408-Countries-Eligible-for-Etsy-Payments) must govern eligibility; never evade country requirements.

## Implications for Studio OS and Antigravity

Studio OS should prioritize evidence, paid validation and product QA before volume. Daily draft automation can operate on authorized briefs, but it must not turn hypotheses into approval or imply automatic revenue. Keep the mobile-detailing package as an internal engineering pilot until validated; do not silently rename it a commercially proven opportunity. Antigravity should read this report, preserve unsupported claims as hypotheses, and build evidence-linked review packages. A pivot in commercial priority belongs to the owner.
